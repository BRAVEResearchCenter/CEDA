## Brief CEDA Description
The following description is a brief overview of the CEDA study. For methodological and sample characteristic details, please refer to published manuscripts.

### Purpose
The goal of this project was to track trajectories of implicit and explicit emotion regulation within the brain and body in healthy adolescents and adolescents with affective disorders with and without a history of maltreatment. 

### Design 
This project utilizes an accelerated longitudinal design, where youth between the ages of 10-17 were followed at a baseline and one year follow-up. Both youth and their parent/caregiver participated, where clinical assessment data, biological samples, brain structure, and brain and body function (via fMRI, skin conductance response, and heart rate variability) were collected during emotion regulation tasks.

### Tasks
***Implicit Emotion Regulation Task.*** 

The Emotional N-Back (EN-Back) task was used to index implicit emotion regulation. Task design parameters and stimuli were taken from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development® (ABCD) Study, and accessed from: https://abcdstudy.org/scientists/abcd-fmri-tasks-and-tools/

***Explicit Emotion Regulation Task.***  

A cognitive reapraisal task developed by Jennifer A. Silvers and colleagues specifically for youth (Silvers et al., 2012) was slightly modified and used to index explicit emotion regulation. Nicknamed the "Perspective Task" in our lab, participants are instructed to either emotionally engage or disengage with the IAPS picture stimuli presented. To emotionally engage, they were instructed to imagine they were close to the scene as if they were physically there, and to naturally experience any emotions that arose. During emotional disengagement, they were instructed to distance themself from the scene, imagining they were viewing the image from a far distance.

> Silvers, J. A., McRae, K., Gabrieli, J. D. E., Gross, J. J., Remy, K. A., & Ochsner, K. N. (2012). Age-related differences in emotional reactivity, regulation, and rejection sensitivity in adolescence. _Emotion, 12_(6), 1235–1247. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028297
